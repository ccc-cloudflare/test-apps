## Overview

Mass-energy equivalence is the famous conclusion of Einstein's special relativity:
**mass and energy are two forms of the same physical quantity**. Its best-known formula is

$$
E = mc^2
$$

where $E$ is energy, $m$ is mass and $c$ is the speed of light in vacuum,
about $299\,792\,458 \,\text{m/s}$.

## A little mass is a lot of energy

Because $c^2$ is such a large number, a tiny mass corresponds to enormous energy:

| Mass | Energy | Roughly equivalent to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $1 \,\text{kg}$ | $9 \times 10^{16} \,\text{J}$ | 21 megatons of TNT |
| $1 \,\text{g}$ | $9 \times 10^{13} \,\text{J}$ | about 21 kilotons of TNT, close to one atomic bomb |

## The full form

For a moving object, total energy obeys

$$
E^2 = \left( m_0 c^2 \right)^2 + (pc)^2
$$

where $p$ is momentum and $m_0$ is the **rest mass**. A particle with zero rest mass
(such as the photon $\gamma$) carries no rest energy but still has momentum and energy $E = pc$.

## In the real world

- Nuclear power and weapons: the mass defect of nuclear reactions turns into energy.
- Stellar fusion: the Sun converts about $4.26 \times 10^9 \,\text{kg}$ of mass into
  radiation every second.
- Particle accelerators: "creating" new particles out of pure energy is $E = mc^2$ in reverse.
